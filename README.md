# Henry's Wild Typing

An offline-first, desktop-oriented typing adventure for Chrome and Edge on Windows and macOS. Vue 3 + Vite PWA + SQLite (`sql.js`, persisted in IndexedDB), following the architecture of `shopping-list`.

## Run

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the localhost URL shown by Vite. For an installable production PWA, run `npm run build && npm run preview`, open the localhost URL in Chrome or Edge, then use the browser's **Install app** action. Load it once while online to cache the app; missions, SQLite progress, trophies, typing tests, and local field reports then work offline. Browser speech synthesis reads letter hints and encouragement using available voices.

In **Settings → Voice & sound**, choose a speech language and one of the voices reported by your browser, then press **Test voice** and **Save settings**. The app remembers your choice on this device. Voices marked *on this device* can work offline; voices marked *may need internet* are supplied by the browser and may need a connection. If a selected online voice is unavailable, the app tries a local voice. The available voices vary by browser and operating system; changing speech language changes pronunciation, not the English game text. Speech does not use the OpenRouter key.

## Missions

Ten progressively harder expeditions use animals featured on Forrest Galante's *Extinct or Alive* and its Shark Week specials. [Episode source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_or_Alive#Episodes). These are fictional learning missions, not accounts of verified modern animal sightings. Each mission includes a briefing, three timed game stages (Travel, Fieldwork and Rescue), a field report, and a trophy. Each game has a testing skip that advances without awarding a stage result. In Travel, type 60 familiar words to fly from Auckland across a self-hosted antique-style world map to the destination. The red dotted trail and plane advance with each word; ten seconds without a correct key or exceeding 150 seconds crashes the plane and restarts the flight. Fieldwork is a 90-second typing race against three poacher vehicles; the camera follows Henry's jeep through scrolling habitat scenery while rivals can drive offscreen and the finish line appears in the final stretch. Every correct key boosts the jeep. Rival vehicles surge and slow independently on randomized routes that reach the finish after 91, 100, and 110 seconds; Henry must reach it in 90 seconds or retry. Both stages award stars based on conventional WPM × accuracy (five stars at 30+ adjusted WPM). Rescue is a 90-second wildlife camera watch: type at the mission's adjusted WPM target (10 in mission 1, increasing by 2 per mission to 28 in mission 10) and 92% recent accuracy to fill the Luck meter, then sustain that pace at green for 15 uninterrupted seconds to reveal the mission animal. If time runs out, the camera records no sighting and the watch can be retried. Camera views rotate between habitat-specific daytime and night-vision scenery every ten seconds. Rescue's five-star target is 2 adjusted WPM above that mission's minimum. A one-minute typing test tracks WPM, accuracy, and WPM × accuracy. Saved data stays in the current browser profile on this device.

Travel, Fieldwork, Rescue, and the typing test begin with a large **3–2–1 countdown**; their timers start only afterward. In Fieldwork, a correct key adds 5 mph to Henry's speed (up to 180), an incorrect key subtracts 1 mph, and after a second without a correct key speed falls smoothly by 2 mph every tenth of a second. The scenery and road keep scrolling as the jeep coasts. Typing the full set of words still determines when Henry reaches the finish, so acceleration does not shorten the challenge.

The app self-hosts **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** so lowercase `l`, uppercase `I`, and the numeral `1` have distinct shapes, including when offline. The antique flight map is generated from [Natural Earth public-domain country boundaries](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) with `node scripts/generate-world-map.mjs` and locally bundled; the camera follows the flight at closer zoom. Animal images are locally bundled, resized Wikimedia Commons photos (or historical specimens and an accurately labeled scientific illustration where live photography is unavailable). Notes distinguish representative relatives and archival images. Credits and license links are in the trophy shelf, with the individual image source also shown in each report; metadata lives in `src/images.ts`. No outside image or font request is needed in offline mode.

## Optional AI

Enter an OpenRouter key and chat model in Settings. Reports can then be checked for spelling, grammar, and punctuation; fix highlighted errors and re-check before sending to receive a fictional, explorer-style reply. Without connectivity or a key the report can be completed with a clearly labeled local reply. Requests are made directly from the browser to OpenRouter; the key is saved locally in SQLite. Browser speech synthesis does not require OpenRouter.

The **20 Questions** mode below Typing Test also uses OpenRouter and requires an internet connection. The AI chooses from a shuffled list of familiar animals, plants (the traditional “vegetable” category), and everyday materials (the “mineral” category). Type up to 20 yes-or-no questions; a single AI call checks spelling and, if there are no corrections, returns Yes, No, or a clarification. Misspellings must be corrected before a question counts. Repeated, unclear, or off-topic questions keep their turn, and hints are available for one turn when fewer than ten questions remain. The app checks turn counts, duplicate questions, valid guesses, and allowed answer choices locally; AI replies are limited to yes/no or a short clarification.

**Story Adventure**, beside 20 Questions, is an online AI pick-a-path game. Choose an original fan adventure inspired by Harry Potter, Dog Man, or Warriors, or a clearly fictional Forrest Galante wildlife expedition. Each scene offers four short worded actions; type one of them or a sensible related alternative. Numbers and single letters are not actions. One AI request checks spelling and continues the story only if the action makes sense. Corrections and unclear actions keep the turn. The guide aims for a reading age of ten, avoids adult or graphic content, and closes the story after 20 accepted turns. A new random seed starts each adventure.

**Dungeon Labyrinth**, also under Typing Test, creates five new 25-room square mazes whenever you start a new game. Explore through typed sentences, read the four suggested actions in each room, peek through doors for clues, ask Stampy the lost talking cat for advice, collect items and gold explicitly, solve word-dial boxes to find some equipment, and use equipment to open dangerous passages. Your map has fog of war and shows doors leading to unexplored rooms; hidden bombable walls lead to optional treasure chambers. Moving into fire, monsters, a guard dog or a canyon without solving the hazard restarts only that level, keeping your map, inventory, collected gold and stones. Pick up one magic stone at the end of each level to meet the unnamed dragon; return its stones, give it a friendly name and say something kind to befriend it. Questions about the dragon get in-character replies, while insulting it restarts the conversation. One OpenRouter call checks spelling and interprets each action; misspellings never change the game. The maze, inventory, hazards and outcomes are enforced locally and saved on this device, so reloading keeps your dungeon. Typing new actions requires a key and internet.

The dungeon map has a labelled **North / East / South / West** compass. Hover over or keyboard-focus a visited, numbered room to recall its description. Type “go to room 12” to follow a safe route through explored rooms on the current level; unexplored rooms and unopened hazards cannot be skipped. The suggested actions sit below the event log, and the action input regains focus after each response. Stampy's grey-cat illustration is bundled for offline use.
